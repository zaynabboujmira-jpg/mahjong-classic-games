Dominoes is one of the simplest games to start and one of the most satisfying to play well. The basic rule fits in a sentence: match the numbers on the ends. But the choice of which tile to play, and when, separates a lucky win from a reliable one. This guide covers the two most popular games, Draw and Block, with the scoring rules and a few tactics.

## The set

The standard set is **double-six**: 28 tiles, each showing two numbers from 0 (blank) to 6. Every combination appears once, from 0-0 to 6-6. Tiles with the same number on both halves, such as 4-4, are **doubles**. Each number appears on 8 tile ends in the set (7 tiles, counting the double twice).

The **pip count** of a tile is the total of its two halves: 6-5 has 11 pips, 0-1 has 1.

## Setting up

1. Shuffle all tiles face down.
2. Each player draws their hand: **7 tiles for two players, 5 tiles each for three or four** players.
3. The remaining tiles stay face down as the **boneyard**.

## Who goes first

A common rule: the player holding the **highest double** (6-6, then 5-5 and so on) plays it to start the line. If nobody has a double, the player with the heaviest tile starts.

## Playing

Players take turns clockwise. On your turn, place one tile at either **open end** of the line so that the touching halves match. If the line shows 3 on one end and 5 on the other, you can play any tile containing a 3 or a 5.

When a tile fits both ends, you choose which end to play it on. Doubles are usually placed crosswise, but they work exactly like other tiles.

## Draw game or Block game

The two games differ only in what happens when you cannot play:

| Rule | Draw game | Block game |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cannot play | Draw from the boneyard until you can play | Pass your turn |
| Boneyard empty | Pass | (No drawing at all) |
| Typical feel | Longer rounds, fewer blocked games | Faster, more tactical, more blocks |

## Ending a round and scoring

A round ends in one of two ways:

1. **A player goes out** (plays their last tile). They score the **total pips left in every other hand**.
2. **The game is blocked**: nobody can play. Everyone counts the pips in their own hand; the player with the **lowest total scores the pips in the other hands**. If the lowest totals tie, nobody scores.

Rounds continue until a player reaches the target score, often **100 points**. Other popular games, such as All Fives (Muggins), score points during play for making the open ends add up to multiples of five, but the basic matching rules are the same.

## Strategy that wins rounds

### Play heavy tiles early

If someone else goes out, the pips in your hand count against you. Getting rid of 6-6, 6-5 and 5-5 early limits the damage when a round goes wrong.

### Keep a variety of numbers

A hand with tiles for many different numbers can play on almost any end. A hand full of 2s gets stuck the moment both ends show something else. When choosing between two plays, keep the hand that covers more numbers.

### Count the numbers

Each number appears on 8 tile ends. If you can see that six 4-ends are already on the table and you hold the seventh, nobody else can play on a 4 except with the last one. Making both open ends show a number your opponents are out of can force them to draw or pass.

### Watch what your opponents cannot play

When an opponent draws or passes, they have no tile for either open end. Remember those numbers and try to keep them showing.

### Doubles are awkward

A double matches only one number and adds no new number to the line. Play doubles when they are safe, rather than being stuck with one at the end.

### In the Block game, think about blocking

Without a boneyard, a blocked game is common. If your hand is light and you can block the game while your opponents hold heavy tiles, ending it on purpose can win the round.

## Quick scoring example

Three players play the Draw game. You go out first. The other two hands hold 6-4 and 2-1 (13 pips), and 5-5 (10 pips). You score 13 + 10 = **23 points**. If instead the game had blocked with you holding 0-1 (1 pip), you would still have the lowest hand and score the others' pips. A few rounds like this reach 100 surprisingly fast, which is why getting rid of heavy tiles early matters.
