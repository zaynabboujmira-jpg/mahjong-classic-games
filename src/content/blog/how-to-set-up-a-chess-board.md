Setting up a chess board takes about a minute once you know the pattern, and getting it right matters: a board turned the wrong way or a swapped king and queen changes the whole game. This guide shows where every piece goes, gives you a checklist to run before the first move, and lists the mistakes that catch beginners most often.

## Step 1: turn the board the right way

A chess board has 64 squares in an 8 by 8 grid, alternating light and dark. Before you place a single piece, check one corner: **each player must have a light square in the bottom-right corner**. The usual memory aid is "light on the right" (or "white on the right").

If the square in your right-hand corner is dark, give the board a quarter turn. Everything else depends on this, so it is the first thing to check.

## Step 2: know the names of the squares

Squares are named by a letter and a number. The **files** (columns) run from **a to h**, left to right from White's side. The **ranks** (rows) run from **1 to 8**, starting at White's back row. White's pieces start on ranks 1 and 2, and Black's on ranks 7 and 8.

You do not need the names to play, but they make the setup easy to describe and they are how chess moves are written down, for example e4 or Nf3.

## Step 3: place the back row

The back row holds the eight pieces that are not pawns. Work from the corners to the middle:

| Square (White) | Square (Black) | Piece |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a1 and h1 | a8 and h8 | Rooks, in the corners |
| b1 and g1 | b8 and g8 | Knights, next to the rooks |
| c1 and f1 | c8 and f8 | Bishops, next to the knights |
| d1 | d8 | Queen |
| e1 | e8 | King |

That gives White, from left to right: **rook, knight, bishop, queen, king, bishop, knight, rook**. Black's back row is the mirror image, so every piece faces its opposite number across the board.

### Queen on her colour

The two middle squares are the ones people mix up. The rule is **queen on her own colour**: the white queen goes on the light square d1, and the black queen goes on the dark square d8. The kings take the remaining middle squares, e1 and e8, and end up directly facing each other on the e-file. If your queens face each other and your kings face each other, the middle is right.

## Step 4: line up the pawns

Each side has eight pawns. Put them all on the row directly in front of the back row: **rank 2 for White and rank 7 for Black**. Pawns are the only pieces that fill a whole row, so this step is quick.

## Setup checklist

Run through this before the first move:

1. A light square is in the bottom-right corner for both players.
2. Rooks are in the four corners.
3. Knights stand next to the rooks, bishops next to the knights.
4. Each queen is on her own colour, on the d-file.
5. Each king is on the e-file, facing the other king.
6. Eight pawns stand on each side's second row.
7. Each side has 16 pieces: 8 pawns, 2 rooks, 2 knights, 2 bishops, 1 queen and 1 king.

Then **White moves first**, and the players take turns from there.

## Common setup mistakes

| Mistake | How to spot it | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Board turned the wrong way | A dark square in your right-hand corner | Rotate the board a quarter turn |
| King and queen swapped | Your queen is not on her own colour, or the queens do not face each other | Put the queen on her colour, the king beside her |
| Knights and bishops swapped | A bishop sits next to a rook | Knights go next to the rooks, bishops inside them |
| Black set up as a copy of White | Black's queen is not opposite White's queen | Black mirrors White: queens on the d-file, kings on the e-file |

The king and queen swap is by far the most common. It even changes which side each player castles on, so it is worth checking twice.

## How each piece moves, in one line each

Once the board is set, here is a quick reminder of what each piece does:

- **Rook:** any number of squares in a straight line along a rank or file.
- **Knight:** an L-shape, two squares one way and one square to the side. It is the only piece that can jump over others.
- **Bishop:** any number of squares diagonally, so it stays on one colour all game.
- **Queen:** any number of squares in any straight line or diagonal.
- **King:** one square in any direction, plus castling.
- **Pawn:** one square forward, or two on its first move, and captures one square diagonally forward.

For castling, en passant and promotion, see the special moves guide: [Chess Special Moves: Castling, En Passant and Promotion](/blog/chess-special-moves-castling-en-passant/).

## What to do after the setup

The first few moves matter more than they look. Take the centre with a pawn, bring out your knights and bishops, and castle early.
