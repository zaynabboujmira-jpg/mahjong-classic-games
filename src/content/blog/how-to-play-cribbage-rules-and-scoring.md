Cribbage is a card game with its own scoreboard: a wooden board full of holes, with pegs that leapfrog towards 121. The rules look busy at first, but every point comes from the same few patterns: cards adding up to fifteen, pairs and runs. This guide walks through a two-player hand from the deal to the count, with the scores in each step.

## What you need

A standard 52-card deck and a cribbage board. Aces are low and count as 1, the face cards (jack, queen, king) count as 10 and every other card counts its number. In runs, the cards go in their natural order from ace up to king, so ace-2-3 is a run but queen-king-ace is not.

Each player has two pegs. Every time you score, you move your back peg in front of your front peg by the number of points scored. The gap between your two pegs always shows your last score, so mistakes are easy to spot. The first player to reach **121**, the game hole, wins.

## The deal and the crib

Cut the deck to decide the first dealer; the lower card deals. The dealer then gives **six cards** to each player, one at a time.

Each player then chooses **two cards to throw into the crib**, a third hand that belongs to the dealer and is scored at the end of the hand. When it is your crib, throw cards that score together, such as a pair or a 7 and an 8. When it is your opponent's crib, throw cards that are far apart and do not make fifteen.

The deal alternates every hand, so the crib does too.

## The starter card

After the discard, the **non-dealer cuts the deck** and the dealer turns up the top card of the lower half. This is the **starter** (or cut card). It is not played, but it counts as a fifth card for both hands and the crib in the show.

If the starter is a jack, the dealer scores 2 at once. This is called **his heels**.

## Pegging: playing to 31

Now the cards are played one at a time, starting with the non-dealer. Each player lays a card face up in front of themselves and says the running total: "five", "fifteen", "twenty-two". The count may never pass **31**.

You score points in the pegging for:

| Play | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Making the count exactly 15 | 2 |
| Making the count exactly 31 | 2 |
| A pair (same rank as the card just played) | 2 |
| Three of a kind (pair royal) | 6 |
| Four of a kind (double pair royal) | 12 |
| A run of three or more among the latest cards, in any order | 1 per card |
| The go, or the last card | 1 |

If you cannot play without passing 31, you say **"go"**. Your opponent then keeps playing any cards that still fit. When neither player can play, the last one to lay a card scores **1 for the go**, the count restarts from zero and the other player leads. Reaching exactly 31 scores 2 instead of 1. Whoever plays the very last card of the hand also scores 1.

Runs in the pegging do not need to be played in order. If the cards go 4, 6, 5, the 5 completes a run of three and scores 3.

## The show: counting your hand

Once all eight cards are played, each player picks up their four cards and counts them together with the starter. The **non-dealer counts first**, then the dealer, then the dealer counts the crib. Score every combination:

- **Fifteens:** each different group of cards adding up to 15 scores 2.
- **Pairs:** each pair scores 2, so three of a kind is three pairs (6) and four of a kind is six pairs (12).
- **Runs:** three or more cards in sequence score 1 per card. If a card in the run is paired, the run counts twice: 7-8-8-9 is a double run of three, worth 6 plus 2 for the pair.
- **Flush:** four cards of one suit in your hand score 4, or 5 if the starter matches too. In the crib, only a five-card flush counts.
- **Nobs:** the jack of the same suit as the starter, in your hand or crib, scores 1.

Take the hand 5-5-6-4 with a jack as the starter. The fifteens are 5+J twice, 5+6+4 twice: 8 points. The pair of 5s is 2 more. The runs 4-5-6 twice are 6 more. The total is 16.

## Winning, skunks and the 29 hand

The game ends the moment a player pegs their 121st point, whether that comes from pegging, the show or the crib. That is why the counting order matters: late in a game, the non-dealer counts first and can win before the dealer counts at all.

A loser who finishes below 91 has been **skunked**, and below 61 **double skunked**. In club play, a skunk counts as two games.

The highest possible hand is **29**: three 5s and the jack in your hand, with the fourth 5 of the jack's suit as the starter. Eight fifteens (16), six pairs (12) and nobs (1).

## Three tips for your first games

1. **Keep your 5s.** A 5 makes fifteen with any 10-card, and there are sixteen of those in the deck.
2. **Lead a low card.** A first card of 4 or lower cannot be answered with a fifteen.
3. **Count out loud.** Saying "fifteen two, fifteen four" for every fifteen keeps you from missing points.
