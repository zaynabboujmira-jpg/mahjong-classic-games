Mahjong is a four-player game of skill, strategy and a little luck, played with tiles instead of cards. It developed in China in the 19th century and is now played all over the world, from Hong Kong parlours to American community centres. The rules below are the common core shared by most styles, so you can sit down at almost any table and follow along.

## What you are trying to do

Each player holds a hand of 13 tiles. On every turn you take one tile and give one away, trying to turn your hand into a **winning hand of 14 tiles: four sets and a pair**. The first player to complete one calls "Mahjong" and wins the hand.

A **pair** is two identical tiles. A **set** is one of three things:

| Set | What it is | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chow | Three tiles in a row in one suit | 4, 5, 6 of bamboo |
| Pung | Three identical tiles | Three red dragons |
| Kong | Four identical tiles | Four 9s of dots |

Honour tiles (winds and dragons) have no numbers, so they can form pungs and kongs but never chows.

## The tiles

A standard Chinese or Hong Kong set has 144 tiles:

- **Three suits, numbered 1 to 9, four of each:** dots, bamboo and characters (108 tiles).
- **Winds:** East, South, West and North, four of each (16 tiles).
- **Dragons:** red, green and white, four of each (12 tiles).
- **Flowers and seasons:** eight bonus tiles, one of each.

If the symbols are new to you, the [Mahjong tiles guide](/blog/mahjong-tiles-meaning-guide/) shows how to read every one.

## Setting up and dealing

The tiles are shuffled face down and built into a square wall. Each player is dealt 13 tiles, and the dealer, called **East**, takes a 14th and starts by discarding. Bonus tiles (flowers and seasons) are not part of a hand: when you draw one, you set it aside and draw a replacement. On a screen all of this happens automatically.

## Taking a turn

Play moves counter-clockwise, from East to the player on East's right, and so on. On your turn:

1. **Draw** a tile from the wall.
2. If your hand is complete, declare **Mahjong**.
3. Otherwise **discard** one tile face up in the middle of the table.

Discards are public, so every player can see what has gone.

## Calling a discard

You do not have to wait for your turn to use a discarded tile. When someone discards, you may **call** it:

- **Pung or Kong:** from any player, if you hold two (or three) matching tiles.
- **Chow:** only from the player directly before you, and only to complete a run.
- **Mahjong:** from any player, if that tile completes your hand.

If several players call the same tile, a win beats a pung or kong, and a pung or kong beats a chow. When you call, you place the finished set face up in front of you, and then discard. A **kong** gives you an extra tile, so you draw a replacement from the wall.

## A sample winning hand

Here is one complete hand of 14 tiles:

- 1, 2 and 3 of dots: a chow
- 7, 8 and 9 of bamboo: a chow
- three East winds: a pung
- three red dragons: a pung
- two 5s of characters: the pair

Four sets and a pair: that is Mahjong. In Hong Kong scoring the dragon pung is worth a faan, and the East pung scores too when East is your seat wind or the wind of the round.

## Winning and scoring

You win by drawing the last tile you need yourself (**self-drawn**) or by calling a discard. If the wall runs out before anyone wins, the hand is a draw.

Scoring is where styles differ most:

- **Hong Kong style** counts **faan** for features such as dragon pungs or one-suit hands, and many tables need a minimum, often 3 faan, before you may declare a win. The [Mahjong scoring guide](/blog/mahjong-scoring-faan-explained/) explains it step by step.
- **Chinese Classic** rules on this site use the same faan, with no minimum, so any complete hand wins.
- **American Mahjong** has no chows. You build one of the hands printed on a yearly card, with jokers and a tile exchange called the Charleston. The [American vs Hong Kong rules guide](/blog/american-vs-hong-kong-mahjong-rules/) compares the two.

## Tips for your first games

- **Keep pairs and near-sets.** Two tiles of a run, such as 4 and 5 of dots, can become a chow from two different tiles.
- **Discard lonely honour tiles early,** unless they are dragons or your own wind, which often score.
- **Watch the discards.** A tile that three players have already thrown away is usually safe to discard.
- **Pick a direction.** Collecting mostly one suit makes your hand easier to finish and, in Hong Kong rules, worth more.
- **Do not call everything.** Each call reveals part of your hand and takes away flexibility.

## Start playing

The easiest way to learn is to play. [Chinese Classic Mahjong](/mahjong/chinese/) on this site has no minimum score, so any four sets and a pair win, which makes it ideal for a first game. When you are comfortable, try [Hong Kong Mahjong](/mahjong/hong-kong/) with flowers and the 3-faan minimum, or [American Mahjong](/mahjong/american/) with jokers and the Charleston. You play against three computer players, and call buttons appear whenever a discard can be claimed.
