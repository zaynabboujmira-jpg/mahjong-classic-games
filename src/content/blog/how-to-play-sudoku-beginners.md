Sudoku looks like a maths puzzle, but it is not: you never add anything up. It is a puzzle of logic, and once you know where to look, even a blank-looking grid starts to fill itself in. This guide explains the rules, the two techniques that solve every easy puzzle, and a simple routine to follow when you are stuck.

## The rules of Sudoku

A Sudoku grid has 81 cells in 9 rows and 9 columns, divided into nine **boxes** of 3×3. Some cells already hold a digit; these are the **givens**, and you cannot change them. Your job is to fill every empty cell so that:

- each **row** contains the digits 1 to 9 exactly once,
- each **column** contains the digits 1 to 9 exactly once,
- each **3×3 box** contains the digits 1 to 9 exactly once.

That is the whole game. A well-made puzzle has exactly one solution, so every digit can be found by reasoning.

## Your first digits: scanning

The quickest way to start is to **scan** for one digit at a time. Pick a digit that appears often in the givens, say 5:

1. Find every 5 already in the grid.
2. Each 5 rules out its whole row, its whole column and its box.
3. Look for a box that has no 5 yet. If only **one cell** in that box is not ruled out, the 5 must go there.

This is often called cross-hatching, because you are crossing out rows and columns. Repeat it for every digit, from 1 to 9, and you will place a good number of digits on an easy puzzle before you need anything else.

## Two techniques that solve easy puzzles

### Naked single

Look at one empty cell and check its row, column and box. If eight different digits already appear there, **the cell can only be the ninth**. That cell is a naked single.

### Hidden single

Now turn it around. Pick a row, column or box and a digit it is missing. If that digit can only go in **one cell** of that row, column or box, place it there, even if the cell itself still has other possible digits. Scanning, as above, is a way of finding hidden singles in boxes.

For example, suppose a row is missing 3, 6 and 8 in three empty cells. If two of those cells already see a 3 in their column or box, the third cell must be the 3, and the other two are left with 6 and 8.

Every Easy puzzle on this site can be solved with naked singles alone, and every Medium puzzle needs hidden singles.

## Pencil marks: never guess

On harder puzzles, scanning runs dry. That is the moment for **pencil marks** (also called notes or candidates): small digits in a cell showing what could still go there. Write them in for every empty cell, then:

- place any cell left with a single candidate,
- remove a candidate from a cell's row, column and box every time you place a digit.

Pencil marks turn a puzzle you would have to guess at into one you can reason through. On a screen, a **Fill notes** button can add every candidate at once.

## A routine for any puzzle

When you sit down to a new Sudoku, follow this order:

1. **Scan** every digit from 1 to 9 for hidden singles in the boxes.
2. **Check the fullest rows, columns and boxes**: with only two or three gaps left, the missing digits are easy to place.
3. **Add pencil marks** once scanning stops working.
4. **Look for singles again** in the pencil marks: one candidate in a cell, or one place for a digit in a unit.
5. If you are still stuck, move on to the techniques in the [advanced Sudoku techniques guide](/blog/sudoku-advanced-solving-techniques/): pairs, pointing pairs and then X-Wing.

## How to check your work

When the grid is full, check each row, column and box once: each should hold nine different digits. If two digits clash, go back to the last step you can prove and work forward from there. In the game on this site, **Show mistakes** marks a wrong digit as soon as you place it, so you can turn it on while you learn and off when you want a pure challenge.

## Common beginner mistakes

| Mistake | Better habit |
| --- | --- |
| Guessing a digit to see if it works | Every step can be proved; look for a single instead |
| Forgetting the boxes and checking only rows and columns | Check all three units for every digit you place |
| Not updating pencil marks after placing a digit | Remove that digit from its row, column and box straight away |
| Starting with a hard puzzle | Build your scanning speed on Easy and Medium first |
