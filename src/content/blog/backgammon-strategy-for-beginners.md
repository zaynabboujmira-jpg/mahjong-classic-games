Backgammon is a race with dice, but luck is only half of it. Two players with the same rolls will not end up in the same place: one builds a wall, keeps their checkers safe and wins the race, the other leaves blots everywhere and gets hit. These eight ideas will make a difference straight away. If you still need the rules, start with [How to Play Backgammon](/blog/how-to-play-backgammon-beginners/).

## 1. Play the standard opening moves

The first roll of the game has well-tested best moves. Learn these and you will start every game well:

| Roll | Move | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3-1 | 8/5, 6/5 | Makes your 5-point, the best point in the game |
| 4-2 | 8/4, 6/4 | Makes your 4-point |
| 6-1 | 13/7, 8/7 | Makes your bar point (the 7-point) |
| 5-3 | 8/3, 6/3 | Makes your 3-point |
| 6-5 | 24/13 | Runs a back checker all the way to safety |

For other rolls, a common plan is to bring one checker down from the midpoint and split or move a back checker, aiming to make a new point next turn.

## 2. Make points, especially the 5-point

A point with **two or more of your checkers** is safe from being hit and blocks your opponent from landing there. The most valuable points early in the game are your **5-point** and your **bar point (7-point)**, because they block your opponent's back checkers and help build your home board. Use rolls that make a point whenever you can.

## 3. Limit your blots

A **blot** is a single checker on a point, and it can be hit. Some blots are worth the risk: a blot placed to make a point next turn, or one far from enemy checkers. Avoid blots where your opponent has many rolls to hit, especially close in front of their checkers. As a rule of thumb, a blot 6 or fewer points in front of an enemy checker is hit by at least 11 of the 36 possible rolls, and often far more.

## 4. Build a prime

A **prime** is a row of blocked points. A full prime of **six points in a row** cannot be passed at all, and even four or five in a row make escape very hard. Build your prime in front of your opponent's back checkers, then roll it forward into your home board. Trapping two checkers behind a prime is one of the strongest positions in the game.

## 5. Get your back checkers moving, or anchor them

Your two checkers on the 24-point have the longest way home and are the easiest to trap. Either **escape them early**, before your opponent builds a wall, or make an **anchor**: a point of your own in your opponent's home board. An anchor on your opponent's 5-point or 4-point (your 20-point or 21-point) is the most useful, because it is hard to block and gives you shots at their checkers later.

## 6. Hit when it helps

Hitting sends an enemy checker to the bar, costing your opponent all the pips it had travelled and a turn to re-enter. Hit when:

- it stops your opponent making a key point,
- the hit checker had travelled far, or
- your home board is strong, so the checker may struggle to come back in.

Be more careful when your own home board is weak or your opponent has a strong board, because a return hit costs you more.

## 7. Count the pips and race when ahead

The **pip count** is the total distance your checkers must travel to bear off. Each side starts at **167**. When the armies have passed each other and there is no more contact, the game is a pure race, and the lower count is ahead. A lead of about 10% is a big advantage.

If you are clearly ahead, **stop fighting**: bring your checkers home and race. If you are well behind, keep contact instead, holding an anchor and waiting for a shot.

## 8. Bear off safely

Once all fifteen checkers are home, you can **bear them off**. If your opponent still has a checker on the bar or in your home board, play safely: clear your highest points first and avoid leaving blots, even if it costs a checker off. A hit at this stage can turn a sure win into a loss. When there is no contact left, take off as many checkers as you can each roll.

## Gammons, and when to take a risk

Winning before your opponent bears off any checker is a **gammon**, worth double. When you have a strong home board and your opponent has checkers on the bar, play boldly for the gammon. When you are behind, protect against one: get a checker home and borne off.

The [backgammon game on this site](/board/backgammon/) shows the pip count for both sides in the toolbar, so you can practise racing decisions every game. Start against Easy, then try Medium and Hard, which look further ahead and make you pay for loose blots more often.
