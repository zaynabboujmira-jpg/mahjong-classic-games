Checkers is one of the simplest board games to learn and one of the hardest to master. There are only a handful of rules: move diagonally, jump to capture, reach the far side to become a king. This guide covers all of them, from setting up the board to the rules that decide close endgames.

## The board and the pieces

Checkers uses the same **8×8 board** as chess, with 64 squares in alternating light and dark colours. Only the **32 dark squares** are used; the pieces never touch the light squares.

Each player has **12 pieces**, usually called **men**, in two contrasting colours. In the United States the colours are often red and black; on this site they are Red and White.

## How to set up checkers

1. **Turn the board** so that each player has a **dark square in the bottom-left corner**. Turn the board 90 degrees if you see a light square there.
2. **Place your 12 pieces** on the dark squares of the **three rows nearest you**: four pieces on each row.
3. **Your opponent does the same** on their three nearest rows.
4. **Leave the two middle rows empty.** Each side's front row should face the empty space, with four dark squares between the armies on each of the middle rows.

Check that each side has exactly 12 pieces, all on dark squares. That is the whole setup.

## How the pieces move

A man moves **one square diagonally forward**, to the left or right, onto an empty dark square. It cannot move straight ahead, sideways or backwards. Players take turns, one move each, and you cannot pass.

The player with the darker pieces traditionally moves first. On this site, Red moves first.

## Jumping to capture

You capture an enemy piece by **jumping over it**: if an enemy piece is diagonally next to yours and the square directly beyond it is empty, jump into that square and remove the enemy piece from the board.

Three rules make jumping the heart of checkers:

- **Jumps are compulsory.** If you can capture, you must. When several captures are available, you may pick any one of them; you do not have to take the one that wins the most pieces.
- **Multi-jumps continue.** If, after landing, the same piece can jump again, it must keep jumping in the same turn. A chain can change direction to zig-zag across the board.
- **Men jump forward only.** Like their moves, a man's captures go forwards. Only kings can capture backwards.

Because capturing is forced, you can use it against your opponent: offer one piece so that the forced jump puts their piece where you can take two in return. [Checkers Strategy: Forced Jumps and Endgame Tactics](/blog/checkers-forced-jumps-and-endgame-tactics/) shows how.

## Crowning a king

When a man reaches the **far row**, the back row on your opponent's side, it is **crowned**. In a real set you place a captured piece of your own colour on top of it, making a double-height king.

A **king moves and jumps diagonally in any direction**, forwards and backwards, still one square at a time (or one jump at a time). If a man reaches the far row in the middle of a multi-jump, it is crowned and the move ends there; it must wait for the next turn to jump as a king.

Kings are worth much more than men, which is why keeping your own back row filled, so your opponent cannot crown, is one of the first habits to learn.

## How to win

You win when your opponent **cannot make a move**. That happens in two ways:

- you have **captured all of their pieces**, or
- every piece they have left is **blocked**, with no legal move or jump.

## When a game is drawn

If neither side can force a win, the players can agree to a draw. Tournament rules also let a player claim a draw after a long run of moves with no capture or crowning. The game on this site declares a draw after **forty moves by each side** without a capture or a new king.

## Checkers rules at a glance

| Rule | Standard American checkers |
| --- | --- |
| Board | 8×8, dark squares only, dark square in each player's bottom-left corner |
| Pieces | 12 per player, 24 in all |
| First move | Darker colour (Red on this site) |
| Men move | One square diagonally forward |
| Captures | Compulsory; multi-jumps must be completed |
| Kings | Crowned on the far row; move and jump in all four diagonal directions, one square at a time |
| Win | Capture every enemy piece or leave the opponent without a move |

International draughts, played on a 10×10 board, has different rules: men capture backwards and kings fly any distance. The rules above are the American and English game.
