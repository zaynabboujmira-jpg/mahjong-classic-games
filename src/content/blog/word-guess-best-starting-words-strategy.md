Word Guess gives you six tries to find a hidden five-letter word. After each guess, a green tile means the right letter in the right place, orange means the letter is in the word but elsewhere, and grey means the letter is not in the word. Your first word matters because it sets up everything after it. This guide covers which words to start with, how to choose your second guess, and how to avoid the endings that cost games.

## What makes a good first word

A strong opening word does three things:

1. **Uses five different letters.** A repeated letter wastes a slot.
2. **Uses common letters.** E, A, R, O, T, L, I, S, N and C appear in a large share of five-letter words.
3. **Puts them in common positions.** S often starts words, E and Y often end them, and R and L often sit second.

## Strong starting words

| Word | Why it works |
| --- | --- |
| SLATE | S at the start, E at the end, and three common consonants and vowels |
| CRANE | Tests C, R and N, with A and E in typical positions |
| TRACE | Same letters as CRATE and CATER, so any match gives position clues |
| CRATE | Very common letters; a good partner for second word LYING or SOUND |
| STARE | S-T start is common; R and E positions are informative |
| RAISE | Three vowels and two of the most common consonants |
| LEAST | Same letters as SLATE with different positions, a good change of view |

All of these are valid guesses in the game. Pick one and stick with it for a while.

## Why not all vowels?

Words like ADIEU and AUDIO find vowels quickly, which feels useful. But most answers contain only one or two vowels, and once you know them, you still have to find three or four consonants. A word like SLATE tests two vowels and three common consonants, which typically cuts the list of possible answers further. Vowel starters are fine, just slightly less efficient on average.

## The second guess

Your second word decides whether you solve in three or four. There are two good approaches:

- **Five new letters.** If your first word gave little (one or two orange or grey everywhere), play a word with five fresh common letters. SLATE then CORNY, or CRANE then SPLIT, together test ten letters in two turns.
- **Use what you found.** If you already have two or more greens or oranges, play a word that keeps those letters (greens in place, oranges moved) and adds new common letters.

## Guess three and beyond

From the third guess, prefer words that could be the answer: every green in place, every orange somewhere else, and no grey letters. The exception is the **probe**. Imagine you have _ATCH with possible answers BATCH, CATCH, HATCH, LATCH, MATCH, PATCH and WATCH. Guessing them one by one can take six turns. A probe like CLAMP tests C, L, M and P at once and usually tells you the answer in one guess.

## Watch for traps

- **Repeated letters.** Answers such as SPEED, LLAMA or EERIE catch players who assume five different letters. If you are stuck with few letters left, consider a double.
- **Similar endings.** Families like -IGHT, -OUND, -ATCH and -ASTE have many members. Use a probe early rather than guessing members one at a time.
- **Orange letters in the same spot.** An orange letter cannot be where you placed it. Do not try it there again.

## Reading the colours carefully

- A **green** letter is fixed; build every later guess around it.
- An **orange** letter is in the word but not in that spot. Move it, and remember every spot where it has already turned orange.
- A **grey** letter is not in the word, with one exception: if you guess a word with two copies of a letter and the answer has one, one copy shows coloured and the other grey. The grey copy only means there is no second one.

Keeping a quick mental list of "where each orange letter cannot go" often cuts the last few candidates from five to one.

## A daily routine

1. Open with the same strong word every day.
2. If it gives fewer than two coloured tiles, play five new letters next.
3. Otherwise, build on the greens and oranges.
4. From guess three, play possible answers, or a probe when a family of words remains.
5. Before the last guess, list every remaining candidate and pick the most common word.

## Daily or unlimited?

The daily word is the same for everyone and changes at local midnight, which makes it a fair way to compare with friends. Unlimited mode gives you a new word whenever you like, which is ideal for practising starting words. Try a week of each opener in unlimited play and keep the one that solves fastest for you. You can also switch to 4 or 6 letters for a change; the same principles apply.
