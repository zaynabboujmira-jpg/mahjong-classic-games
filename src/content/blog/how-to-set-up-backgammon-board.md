Before the first roll, each player's fifteen checkers go on just four points. Get those four right and the board is ready. This guide explains how the points are numbered, where every checker goes, which way each side moves and how to check the setup in a few seconds. For the full rules of play, see [How to Play Backgammon: Rules for Beginners](/blog/how-to-play-backgammon-beginners/).

## The parts of the board

A backgammon board has **24 long triangles called points**, twelve on each side, split into four quarters of six by a raised strip in the middle called **the bar**.

- Your **home board** is the quarter where your checkers finish. Your opponent's home board is the quarter directly across from it.
- The two other quarters are the **outer boards**.

Points are numbered **from each player's own side**. Your 1-point is the deepest point of your home board, in the corner, and the numbers rise around the board to your 24-point, the deepest point of your opponent's home board. Your opponent numbers the same points the other way, so your 24-point is their 1-point, and your 13-point is their 12-point.

## Where the 15 checkers go

| Your point | Where it is | Checkers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 24-point | Corner of your opponent's home board | 2 |
| 13-point (the midpoint) | Corner of your opponent's outer board, on your side | 5 |
| 8-point | Your outer board, two points from the bar | 3 |
| 6-point | Your home board, next to the bar | 5 |

That makes 2 + 5 + 3 + 5 = 15. Your opponent places their checkers on the mirror-image points from their own side.

## Step by step

1. **Pick a side and a colour.** Sit across from your opponent and agree where the two home boards will be, either both on the left or both on the right.
2. **Two back checkers.** Put 2 of your checkers on your 24-point, in the far corner of your opponent's home board.
3. **Five on the midpoint.** Put 5 on your 13-point, the corner point of your opponent's outer board on your side of the table.
4. **Three on the 8-point.** Put 3 on your 8-point, in your own outer board, two points away from the bar.
5. **Five on the 6-point.** Put 5 on your 6-point, the first point of your home board next to the bar.
6. **Mirror it.** Your opponent does the same from their side. Their two back checkers sit in your home board, on your 1-point.

## The 2-5-3-5 checklist

Before the first roll, check:

- Each side has exactly 15 checkers on the board and none on the bar.
- Your 2 back checkers are on the corner point of your opponent's home board.
- Your 5 midpoint checkers are directly across the board from your opponent's 5 on the 6-point.
- Your 3 checkers on the 8-point are two points from the bar, outside your home board.
- Your 5 checkers on the 6-point are next to the bar, inside your home board.
- Both home boards are on the same side of the table, facing each other.

A quick way to check is to look at the pattern of piles: **2, 5, 3, 5** for each colour, with every pile facing an enemy pile of a different size.

## Which way the checkers move

You move **from your 24-point down towards your 1-point**, around the board in a horseshoe: from your opponent's home board, through their outer board, across to your outer board and into your home board. Your opponent moves the opposite way.

Once all fifteen of your checkers are in your home board, you can start **bearing off**, removing them from the board. The first player to bear off all fifteen wins.

## Common setup mistakes

| Mistake | How to spot it | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A checker missing | A pile of 4 instead of 5 | Count each side to 15 |
| Home boards on opposite sides | The armies do not face each other | Both home boards on the same side |
| 8-point and 6-point swapped | The 3 sit inside the home board | The 5 go next to the bar, the 3 two points further out |
| Back checkers on the wrong corner | Your 2 are in your own home board | They start in your opponent's home board |

## Starting the game

Each player rolls one die. The higher number moves first and plays **both numbers rolled**: if you roll a 6 and your opponent a 1, you play 6-1, which makes your 7-point (the bar point), one of the best opening moves. If both dice match, roll again. After that, players take turns rolling both dice.
