Spades is the classic partnership card game: easy to learn in one hand, with enough bidding and teamwork to keep you playing for years. This guide covers the rules from the deal to the last trick. For a deep dive into bidding, Nil and bags, read [Spades Bidding Explained](/blog/spades-bidding-nil-and-bags-explained/).

## Players, partners and cards

Spades is played by **four players in two teams**. Partners sit **opposite each other**, so the turn always alternates between the teams. You need a standard **52-card deck** with no jokers.

Cards rank from **ace (high)** down to **2 (low)** in every suit. **Spades are always trumps**: any spade beats any card of another suit.

## The deal

Choose a dealer by any method; the deal then passes to the left after every hand. The dealer shuffles and deals the whole deck one card at a time, clockwise, so each player has **13 cards**.

Sort your hand by suit. Before the bidding, count your likely winners: aces, kings with another card of the suit, and your spades.

## Bidding

Starting with the player to the dealer's left and going clockwise, each player bids **how many tricks they expect to win**, from 0 to 13. Each player bids once. Your bid and your partner's bid are added together to make your **team's contract**.

A bid of zero is called **Nil**. It is a promise to win no tricks at all, and it scores or loses a large bonus on its own. A good first rule is to bid the tricks you are confident about, not the ones you hope for. [Spades Bidding Explained](/blog/spades-bidding-nil-and-bags-explained/) shows how to count a hand trick by trick.

## Playing a trick

The player to the dealer's left **leads** the first trick by playing any card except a spade. Then, clockwise, every player plays one card:

1. **Follow suit if you can.** If a heart is led and you hold a heart, you must play a heart.
2. **If you cannot follow suit,** you may play any card: either throw away a low card of another suit or **trump** with a spade.
3. **The trick is won** by the highest spade played. If no spade was played, it is won by the highest card of the suit that was led.

The winner of each trick collects the four cards, places them face down in front of them and leads the next trick. Play continues until all 13 tricks have been played.

## Breaking spades

You may not **lead** a spade until spades have been **broken**, which means a player has played a spade on a trick because they could not follow suit. After that, anyone may lead spades.

The one exception: if the leader's hand contains **only spades**, they may lead one even before spades are broken.

## Scoring in brief

At the end of the hand, each team counts its tricks:

| Result | Score |
| --- | --- |
| Team wins at least its contract | 10 × the bid, plus 1 per extra trick |
| Team wins fewer tricks than its contract | −10 × the bid |
| Each extra trick (a bag) | +1, but every 10 bags cost 100 |
| Successful Nil | +100 for the team |
| Failed Nil | −100 for the team |

For example, a team that bids 5 and wins 7 tricks scores 52: 50 for the contract and 2 bags. The first team to reach **500 points** wins. Many groups also end the game if a team falls to −200. Bags, Nil and the reasons behind them are explained in detail in the [bidding guide](/blog/spades-bidding-nil-and-bags-explained/).

## Spades for 2 players

With two players there are no partnerships. Instead of dealing, players **draw** their hands:

1. Shuffle and place the deck face down between you.
2. The first player looks at the top card. They may **keep it**, then look at the next card and discard it face down, or **discard it** face down and keep the next card instead.
3. Players alternate until the deck runs out, so each player ends up with **13 cards** and 26 are out of play.

Each player then bids on their own, and the normal rules of play apply. Because half the deck is missing, bidding is harder and Nil is riskier.

## Spades for 3 players

Three players usually play **cutthroat**, each for themselves. Remove one card (often the 2 of clubs) and deal **17 cards** each. Everyone bids alone and scores their own contract, bags and Nil, with the same rules for following suit and breaking spades.

## Common house rules

Spades is played with many local variations. Agree on them before the first hand:

- **2 of clubs leads** the first trick instead of the player to the dealer's left.
- **Jokers** or the 2 of spades are added as the highest trumps.
- **Blind Nil**: bidding Nil before looking at your cards, for a bonus of 200.
- **A minimum team bid** of 4 tricks, so a team cannot bid too low.

## Play Spades with a computer partner

The [Spades game on this site](/cards/spades/) deals four hands and seats you opposite a computer partner, against two computer opponents. Only the cards you are allowed to play are lit, so following suit and breaking spades become automatic, and the game suggests a bid while you learn. If you like trick-taking games, try [Hearts](/cards/hearts/), where the aim is to avoid tricks instead.
