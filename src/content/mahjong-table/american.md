## How American Mahjong is different

American Mahjong is played with 152 tiles: the usual suits, winds and dragons, eight flowers and eight **jokers**. Instead of any four sets and a pair, you must build one of the hands printed on a **card**, and the hand has to match exactly. There are no chows: you can only call a discard to expose a group of identical tiles, or to win.

Here, the three suits are called Dots, Bams and Craks. Each suit has its own dragon: the White Dragon (Soap) goes with Dots, Green with Bams and Red with Craks. Hands on the card such as "a kong of the third suit's dragon" use that pairing.

## Reading the card

Every hand is written as groups of tiles. **FF** is a pair of flowers; **1111** is a kong of 1s; colours mark different suits, so three groups in three colours mean three different suits. Numbers on most lines are examples: "like numbers" can be any number, and "consecutive" runs can start anywhere they fit. The game lists the three hands closest to your tiles whenever you open the card.

## The Charleston, step by step

1. **First Charleston** (required): pass three tiles to the right, then three across, then three to the left.
2. **Second Charleston** (optional): if you agree, pass left, across, then right.
3. **Courtesy pass**: swap up to three tiles with the player across; you both pass the smaller number.

Use the Charleston to commit to a section of the card. If you hold several dragons and winds, aim for Winds & Dragons; if your tiles are mostly odd, look at 13579.

## Strategy tips

- **Keep your jokers.** They are the most flexible tiles in the game, and the bots will happily take a joker from your exposures if you give them the chance.
- **Stay flexible early.** Pick two or three hands that share tiles and narrow down as you draw.
- **Expose with care.** Every exposure tells the table what you are building and locks you into hands that contain that group.
- **Watch the swaps.** If you hold the real tile for a joker in someone's exposure, take the joker on your turn.
