## Logic that draws a picture

A nonogram is a grid with numbers along the top and side. Each number is a run of filled cells in that row or column. Work out which cells must be filled and which must stay empty, and a picture appears. Every puzzle here is generated from its number and proved solvable without guessing.

## Solving techniques

- **Overlap.** If a run is longer than half the line, part of it is certain. In a line of 10, a run of 7 always covers cells 4 to 7.
- **Complete lines.** When the clue and its gaps fill the whole line (like "5 4" in 10 cells), fill it at once.
- **Edges.** A filled cell next to the edge starts the first run; fill the rest of that run and cross the cell after it.
- **Cross what is done.** When a line's clue is satisfied, mark the remaining cells empty. They often unlock the crossing lines.
- **Zero means empty.** A clue of 0 means the whole line is empty.

## Three sizes

5×5 puzzles take a minute and teach the rules. 10×10 is the classic size. 15×15 puzzles take longer and reward careful marking; the thicker lines every five cells help you count.
