## Beyond singles

In a hard puzzle you will reach a point where no digit can be placed directly. The answer is to **remove candidates** until a single appears. That is why notes matter: press **Fill notes** once and keep the pencil marks tidy as you go.

## The techniques you will need

**Locked candidates (pointing).** In a box, if every note for a 4 lies in the same row, the 4 for that box must be in that row. No other cell of the row outside the box can be a 4.

**Locked candidates (claiming).** In a row, if every note for a 4 lies inside one box, the rest of that box cannot hold a 4.

**Naked pairs and triples.** Two cells in a unit with the notes {3, 8} and {3, 8} lock up both digits. Remove 3 and 8 from every other cell in that unit. Three cells whose notes use only three digits work the same way.

**Hidden pairs and triples.** If the digits 2 and 6 can only go in the same two cells of a unit, those cells hold 2 and 6, and every other note in those two cells can be removed.

## Strategy tips

- **Always clear singles first.** They are the cheapest progress, and each one may expose a pair.
- **Scan boxes for pointing pairs.** They are the most common hard-level step and are quick to spot once notes are filled in.
- **Work in one unit at a time.** Count how many cells each digit can go in. A digit with two places in two units often points somewhere useful.
