## Sudoku in one sentence

Fill the grid so every row, column and 3×3 box holds the digits 1 to 9 once each. That is the whole rule: no maths, no guessing, just logic.

## How easy puzzles work

Easy puzzles start with about 38 digits already in place, and at every point there is at least one cell where **only one digit can fit**. This is called a *naked single*. To find one, pick an empty cell and cross off every digit already in its row, column and box. If only one digit is left, that is the answer.

Each digit you place rules out more options elsewhere, so the puzzle keeps opening up as you go.

## Tips for your first games

- **Scan the busiest areas first.** Rows, columns and boxes with many digits already filled leave the fewest options for the cells that remain.
- **Use the number pad counts.** The small number under each digit shows how many are still to place. A digit with only one or two left is often easy to finish.
- **Tap a digit in the grid** to highlight every copy of it. The gaps in the pattern show where that digit can still go.
- **Try notes when you are unsure.** Switch Notes on to pencil in small candidates instead of committing to a digit.

When easy puzzles feel automatic, move on to [Medium Sudoku](/sudoku/medium/), which needs one more technique.
